## Where QAOA loses

- **Full QAOA never beats NN + 2-opt**: worse on 2 of 15 runs, better on 0, tied on 13. NN + 2-opt alone is already optimal on 5 of 5 instances at N = 4.
- **Runtime:** full QAOA takes 14.7 s median per instance vs 0.23 ms for NN + 2-opt (~77,394x slower), on a simulator, before any hardware queue or noise.
- **Against random sampling with the same number of candidates** (best of k uniform random tours, k = QAOA's feasible shots): QAOA is better in 0, equal in 13, worse in 2 of 15 runs. At N = 4 there are only 24 orders, so a few dozen feasible samples already hit the optimum by chance; QAOA's best-sample cost is not evidence of quantum advantage.
- **Tour quality inside the QAOA distribution is near random:** the mean feasible sampled tour is 1.138x optimal (median over runs) vs 1.142x for a uniformly random tour. QAOA mostly learns the constraints (feasibility), not the cost.
- **Scaling:** the full QUBO needs N^2 qubits - N=4: 16, N=5: 25, N=6: 36, N=7: 49, N=8: 64. Nothing above N = 4 was simulated; see `qaoa_resources_vs_n.png` for CX counts (all-to-all connectivity, no routing).
- **QAOA windows vs NN + 2-opt:** worse on 16, better on 0, equal on 59 of 75 runs.
- **QAOA windows vs the same decomposition solved exactly:** the same windows solved by enumerating 3! orders (`window_exact`, microseconds) reach the same cost on 74 of 75 runs and a better one on 1.

`amplification` = P(optimal) / uniform P(optimal), where uniform is (number of optimal orders) / 2^(N^2) - the chance a random bitstring is an optimal tour. It mixes two effects, so they are split out: `feasible enrichment` = P(feasible) / (N!/2^(N^2)) and `conditional amp.` = P(optimal | feasible) / (optimal orders / N!). A conditional amplification near 1 means that, once a sample is a valid order, QAOA picks the optimal one no more often than a random valid order would - the gain is in satisfying the constraints, not in the tour cost.
